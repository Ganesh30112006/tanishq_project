WHAT THIS FILE IS

A list of published work that has already studied the specific problems this
module is working on: reading laboratory reports out of PDFs and scans,
running a second reader silently before it is allowed to act, deciding when a
machine should refuse to answer, writing an annotation specification before
labelling starts, and reconciling units and reference ranges across
laboratories.

It is organised by the engineering question it answers, not by year or venue,
so each group can be read on its own when that part of the system is being
worked on.

HOW EACH ENTRY IS MARKED

  [VERIFIED]  I opened the article or its abstract page and read the title,
              authors, venue and year off it. Details below are as printed.

  [LISTING]   The publisher blocked automated access. Title, venue and URL
              come from the search index only. Open it and confirm the
              citation before putting it in a report or a paper. Authors are
              left out where I could not read them.

Nothing in this file is invented. Where a number is quoted, it is from the
abstract or summary of the paper named directly above it.

1. SILENT / SHADOW EVALUATION OF CLINICAL AI
   Directly about the phase this project is in right now.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

[1.1] [VERIFIED]
The silent trial - the bridge between bench-to-bedside clinical AI
applications
Kwong JCC, Erdman L, Khondker A, Skreta M, Goldenberg A, McCradden MD,
Lorenzo AJ, Rickard M
Frontiers in Digital Health, 2022; volume 4
DOI 10.3389/fdgth.2022.929508
https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/digital-health/articles/10.3389/fdgth.2022.929508/full

  WHY IT MATTERS HERE
  This is the closest published description of what DOCUMENT_INTELLIGENCE_MODE
  = shadow is doing. The model runs prospectively on real patients while
  clinicians stay blinded to its output. The authors report a case where model
  performance went from 50 percent to 91 percent AUROC after the silent phase
  exposed problems that retrospective validation had not. That is the argument
  for treating the first batch as failure discovery rather than measurement.

[1.2] [LISTING]
A scoping review of silent trials for medical artificial intelligence
Nature Health, 2025
https://www.nature.com/articles/s44360-025-00048-z

  WHY IT MATTERS HERE
  The review of how silent trials are actually run in practice, and it reports
  that there are no formal guidelines for them. Useful as the citation for why
  the shadow-pilot runbook in docs/shadow-pilot.md had to be written from
  first principles rather than adopted from a standard.

[1.3] [LISTING]
Prospective Evaluation of a 90-day Mortality Prediction Model: From Silent
Pilots to Real Time Deployment in the EHR
medRxiv preprint, 2023
https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2023.01.25.23284977.full.pdf

  WHY IT MATTERS HERE
  A worked account of the silent-pilot-to-deployment path, including the
  pipeline and cohort faults that only appear at inference time. The failure
  class it describes - a cohort definition that used criteria observable only
  after the fact - is the same class as a classifier stratum that turns out
  empty when the real batch runs.

[1.4] [LISTING]
STEP: A Modular Silent Trial Engine for Operational Evaluation of Digital
Pathology AI in Routine Workflow
arXiv:2608.28708
https://arxiv.org/html/2608.28708

  WHY IT MATTERS HERE
  A silent-trial harness built as infrastructure rather than as a one-off
  study. Worth comparing against the ReaderComparison / ReaderDisagreement
  models and the shadow_disagreements command, which are the same idea in a
  different domain.

2. THE SECOND READER, DISAGREEMENT AND ARBITRATION
   The evidence base behind "unsafe disagreements per 100 reports".
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

[2.1] [VERIFIED]
AI-integrated Screening to Replace Double Reading of Mammograms: A
Population-wide Accuracy and Feasibility Study
Elhakim MT, Stougaard SW, Graumann O, Nielsen M, Gerke O, Larsen LB,
Rasmussen BSB
Radiology: Artificial Intelligence, 2024; 6(6)
DOI 10.1148/ryai.230529
https://pubs.rsna.org/doi/full/10.1148/ryai.230529

  WHY IT MATTERS HERE
  The single most relevant empirical result for the second-reader design.
  249,402 screening mammograms, three simulated integrations against standard
  double reading with arbitration. Replacing the FIRST reader kept cancer
  detection and cut screening volume. Replacing the SECOND reader lowered
  sensitivity by 1.58 percent.

  That asymmetry is the published form of the rule this project already
  follows: a second reader that ADDS conservatism is safe, and a second reader
  that REPLACES the existing one can remove safety even while its own accuracy
  looks fine. It is the reason the shadow reader may hold and may never
  release.

[2.2] [VERIFIED]
AI as a Second Reader Can Reduce Radiologists' Workload and Increase Accuracy
in Screening Mammography
Suri A
Radiology: Artificial Intelligence, 2024; 6(6) - invited commentary on [2.1]
DOI 10.1148/ryai.240624
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11605140/

  WHY IT MATTERS HERE
  Short, and it makes the arbitration point explicitly: once one of the two
  readers is a machine, the arbitrator is no longer comparing two human
  opinions and the adjudication task itself changes. That is exactly the
  problem the disagreement taxonomy and the "named reviewer plus written
  reason" requirement in classify() are trying to make tractable.

[2.3] [LISTING]
Artificial intelligence as a second reader for screening mammography
Radiology Advances, 2024; 1(2), umae011
https://academic.oup.com/radadv/article/1/2/umae011/7667296

  WHY IT MATTERS HERE
  A second independent treatment of the same question, useful as a cross-check
  on [2.1] rather than as a new argument.

[2.4] [LISTING]
A novel workflow for the safe and effective integration of AI as supporting
reader in double reading breast cancer screening: A large-scale retrospective
evaluation
medRxiv preprint, 2022
https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2022.06.22.22276751.full.pdf

  WHY IT MATTERS HERE
  The word in the title is SUPPORTING reader, not replacement reader. Read it
  for the workflow design - where the machine's opinion enters, and what
  happens to a disagreement - rather than for the accuracy numbers.

3. ABSTENTION, SELECTIVE PREDICTION AND WHAT REFUSING COSTS
   The literature behind classification_margin_not_met and values_forgone.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

[3.1] [VERIFIED]
Conformal selective prediction with cost aware deferral for safe clinical
triage under distribution shift
Kwon H, Kim D-J
Scientific Reports, 2026
DOI 10.1038/s41598-026-40637-w
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13022086/

  WHY IT MATTERS HERE
  The formal version of what the abstention-cost measurement is groping
  towards. Deferral is given an explicit cost, thresholds are chosen by
  minimising clinical harm on held-out data rather than by maximising
  accuracy, and the risk-coverage tradeoff is tracked under distribution
  shift. The risk-coverage curve is the right shape for the trade the 2.0
  margin is making: coverage is the classified fraction, risk is the unsafe
  disagreement rate.

[3.2] [LISTING]
Abstaining Machine Learning - Philosophical Considerations
arXiv:2409.00706
https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.00706

  WHY IT MATTERS HERE
  Distinguishes AMBIGUITY rejection (abstain where mistakes are likely) from
  NOVELTY rejection (abstain where the input is unlike anything seen). The two
  abstention reasons in the classifier map onto these almost exactly:
  classification_margin_not_met is ambiguity rejection, and
  no_recognised_keywords is novelty rejection. Worth reading before the reason
  codes are extended, because the two want different remedies - a margin
  policy fixes the first, wider keyword coverage fixes the second.

[3.3] [LISTING]
Uncertainty-Driven Reliability: Selective Prediction and Trustworthy
Deployment in Modern Machine Learning
arXiv:2508.07556
https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.07556

  WHY IT MATTERS HERE
  A recent survey. Use it to find the primary sources for whichever abstention
  mechanism is chosen later, rather than as an argument in itself.

[3.4] [LISTING]
Interpretable and Fair Mechanisms for Abstaining Classifiers
arXiv:2503.18826
https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.18826

  WHY IT MATTERS HERE
  Addresses a question this project will eventually have to answer: abstention
  is not free and it is not evenly distributed. If the classifier abstains
  disproportionately on one laboratory's layout or on scanned reports, that is
  a coverage gap that falls on particular patients. The --coverage strata are
  the place that would show up.

4. EXTRACTING VALUES FROM LABORATORY REPORTS AND SCANNED DOCUMENTS
   Prior art for the parser itself.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

[4.1] [VERIFIED]
Extracting laboratory test information from paper-based reports
Ma M-W, Gao X-S, Zhang Z-Y, Shang S-Y, Jin L, Liu P-L, Lv F, Ni W, Han Y-C,
Zong H
BMC Medical Informatics and Decision Making, 2023; 23:251
DOI 10.1186/s12911-023-02346-6
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10629084/

  WHY IT MATTERS HERE
  The closest published system to this one. An OCR module feeding an
  information-extraction module, extracting exactly the four entities this
  parser extracts: test item name, test result, measurement unit, and
  reference value range. Reported averaged OCR accuracy 0.93 and overall IE
  F1 0.86 (precision 0.90, recall 0.83) on 153 reports, at about 0.78 seconds
  per report on CPU.

  Two things to take from it. First, the IE module has MULTI-COLUMN handling
  and line normalisation as named stages - the same column-layout problem that
  produced the censored-value precedence bug here. Second, 0.86 F1 is what a
  published, tuned, CRF-based pipeline scores on this task; that is the honest
  reference point for what "good" looks like, and it is a long way from
  perfect.

[4.2] [VERIFIED]
Deep learning-based NLP data pipeline for EHR-scanned document information
extraction
Hsu E, Malagaris I, Kuo Y-F, Sultana R, Roberts K
JAMIA Open, 2022; 5(2), ooac045
DOI 10.1093/jamiaopen/ooac045
https://academic.oup.com/jamiaopen/article/5/2/ooac045/6605916

  WHY IT MATTERS HERE
  Image preprocessing (greyscale, dilation and erosion, contrast enhancement)
  then Tesseract then classification. Two findings are directly usable: adding
  DOCUMENT LAYOUT information - word position coordinates and page numbers -
  significantly improved performance, and the extraction was framed as a
  three-way classification over candidate numeric values ("is this number an
  AHI value, an SaO2 value, or neither") rather than as free extraction.

  That second framing is worth holding onto when schema-free extraction comes
  up. Their answer to "which number on the page is the result" was to classify
  candidates, which is close to what the _SECOND_NUMBER guard is doing by
  refusing rather than guessing.

[4.3] [LISTING]
Leveraging large language models for structured information extraction from
pathology reports
arXiv:2502.12183
https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.12183

  WHY IT MATTERS HERE
  For the later LLM phase, not now. Read it against the grounding requirement:
  what it does about values the model produces that are not on the page.

[4.4] [VERIFIED]
Measuring the sensitivity of LLM-based structured extraction to prompt,
model, and schema choices in clinical discharge summaries
Murin M
arXiv:2606.05970, 4 June 2026
https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05970

  WHY IT MATTERS HERE
  Read this one before any schema-free extraction work. Its central finding is
  that the largest source of inconsistency was SCHEMA DESIGN - specifically
  the distinction between documented absence and silence - and not prompt
  wording or model size. It also found that a larger model "raises agreement
  on some fields and lowers it on others", so model choice redistributes
  disagreement rather than removing it.

  Documented absence against silence is already a distinction in the frozen
  annotation spec (ABSENT against NOT_FOUND). This paper is empirical evidence
  that getting that distinction right matters more than anything else that
  will be tuned later.

[4.5] [LISTING]
Using natural language processing to extract information from clinical text
in electronic medical records for populating clinical registries: a
systematic review
Journal of the American Medical Informatics Association, 2026; 33(2):484
https://academic.oup.com/jamia/article/33/2/484/8287208

  WHY IT MATTERS HERE
  A current systematic review of the whole field, including the finding that
  regular expressions remain the main technique used in rule-based clinical
  extraction. Good for placing this parser in context and for finding
  comparable systems.

5. OCR QUALITY AND ITS EFFECT DOWNSTREAM
   Behind "don't assume availability means correctness" and the OCR stratum.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

[5.1] [LISTING]
Evaluating and mitigating the impact of OCR errors on information retrieval
International Journal on Digital Libraries, 2023
DOI 10.1007/s00799-023-00345-6
https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00799-023-00345-6

  WHY IT MATTERS HERE
  Documents that OCR errors propagate into downstream tasks - retrieval,
  entity recognition, event detection - rather than staying local. The
  cascading-error argument is the reason OCR_FAILURE is its own class in the
  disagreement taxonomy instead of being folded into PARSER_ERROR.

[5.2] [LISTING]
Rethinking OCR Evaluation for Information Extraction in Business Documents
Springer, 2025
DOI 10.1007/978-981-95-4861-3_21
https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-981-95-4861-3_21

  WHY IT MATTERS HERE
  The important claim: character error rate and word error rate do not reflect
  the impact of OCR noise on extraction, because they weigh every character
  equally and extraction does not. A single corrupted digit in a haemoglobin
  value and a corrupted letter in a page footer are the same CER and are not
  the same event. If OCR quality is ever reported for this pipeline, this is
  the argument for reporting it per extracted value, not per character.

[5.3] [LISTING]
Confidence-Aware Document OCR Error Detection
arXiv:2409.04117
https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.04117

  WHY IT MATTERS HERE
  On using the OCR engine's own confidence to predict where it went wrong.
  Relevant to the ocr_confidence value that is already threaded through
  validate_value, and to whether that number deserves more weight than it
  currently gets.

6. UNITS, LOINC AND REFERENCE RANGES
   Behind UnitRelation: SAME_UNIT / CONVERTIBLE / INCOMPATIBLE / UNKNOWN.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

[6.1] [VERIFIED - authors confirmed from the index; the article page is blocked]
Automated standardization and harmonization of laboratory units in
large-scale clinical data using open-source R functions
Zayed AM, Sarikakis I, Delvaux N
International Journal of Medical Informatics, 2025 (Epub 1 October 2025)
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41061385/

  WHY IT MATTERS HERE
  The largest reported attempt at exactly the problem the unit-relation work
  is solving. On 163.9 million LOINC-mapped quantitative results: 96.2 percent
  of records standardised, unit heterogeneity reduced by 81 percent, 99.97
  percent correctness of standardised units on validation, and 83.4 percent of
  standardised records harmonised to reference units.

  Note what those numbers mean read the other way. Even with LOINC codes
  already attached and a deterministic UCUM mapping, roughly 4 percent of
  records could not be standardised at all. That residue is the population the
  UNKNOWN relation exists for.

[6.2] [LISTING]
Harmonizing Logical Observation Identifiers Names and Codes (LOINC) Codes and
Units in Real-World Oncology Data: Method Development and Evaluation
JMIR Medical Informatics, 2026; e81254
https://medinform.jmir.org/2026/1/e81254

  WHY IT MATTERS HERE
  Method paper on doing code and unit harmonisation together rather than
  separately. Also carries the figure that 6 to 19 percent of laboratory tests
  cannot be accurately mapped to LOINC - a useful ceiling to know about before
  any LOINC work is proposed for this project.

[6.3] [LISTING]
Scalable Unit Harmonization in Medical Informatics via Bayesian-Optimized
Retrieval and Transformer-Based Re-ranking
arXiv:2505.00810 (also International Journal of Medical Informatics, 2025)
https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.00810

  WHY IT MATTERS HERE
  A learned approach to unit matching. Read it for the failure cases it
  reports rather than the architecture: the units it cannot resolve are likely
  to be the same ones a deterministic matcher cannot resolve.

[6.4] [LISTING]
LUMA: A Mapping Assistant for Standardizing the Units of LOINC-Coded
Laboratory Tests
Applied Sciences, 2022; 12(12):5848
https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/12/12/5848

  WHY IT MATTERS HERE
  An ASSISTANT, not an automaton - a human confirms the mapping. That is the
  same posture as refusing to convert lakh/microlitre automatically and
  sending it to a person instead.

[6.5] [LISTING - could not open the page, reCAPTCHA; confirm before citing]
Recommendations on Measurement Units - Why and How
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6803776/

  WHY IT MATTERS HERE
  Background on why laboratories report the units they do, and why the same
  analyte legitimately carries different units and different reference
  intervals at different laboratories depending on assay, instrument and
  reference population. This is the clinical justification for never
  normalising a printed reference range by applying a conversion factor.

7. ANNOTATION SPECIFICATIONS, GOLD STANDARDS AND AGREEMENT
   Behind the frozen spec, the fingerprint, and the refusal to report
   agreement percentage as the headline number.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

[7.1] [VERIFIED]
Building Gold Standard Corpora for Medical Natural Language Processing Tasks
Deleger L, Li Q, Lingren T, Kaiser M, Molnar K, Stoutenborough L, Kouril M,
Marsolo K, Solti I
AMIA Annual Symposium Proceedings, 2012; pages 144-153
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3540456/

  WHY IT MATTERS HERE
  The practical account of writing annotation guidelines before annotating,
  and of aligning the schema with existing standards so the corpus is reusable
  afterwards. It reports high agreement (F-measures 0.8467 to 0.9176) and is
  still candid that guidelines had to be developed iteratively - which is the
  argument for freezing with a VERSION and a fingerprint rather than freezing
  forever.

[7.2] [VERIFIED]
Counting on Consensus: Selecting the Right Inter-annotator Agreement Metric
for NLP Annotation and Evaluation
James J
arXiv:2603.06865, 6 March 2026 (revised 1 April 2026)
https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.06865

  WHY IT MATTERS HERE
  Directly supports the position already taken here. It organises agreement
  measures by task type, shows how label imbalance and missing data distort
  them, and argues for reporting DISAGREEMENT ANALYSIS alongside any agreement
  figure. That is the published form of "two readers can agree while both are
  wrong": the number is not the finding, the classified disagreements are.

  Note also the label-imbalance point. For named entity recognition, kappa is
  inflated by the large mass of unannotated tokens - which is exactly the
  shape of lab-value extraction, where most of the page is not a result.

[7.3] [LISTING]
Inter-Annotator Reliability of Medical Events, Coreferences and Temporal
Relations in Clinical Narratives by Annotators with Varying Levels of
Clinical Expertise
AMIA Annual Symposium Proceedings, 2012
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3540452/

  WHY IT MATTERS HERE
  Measures how much annotator clinical expertise changes the labels. Relevant
  to the adjudication design: who is allowed to classify a disagreement is a
  measurable variable, not an administrative detail, which is why
  classified_by is a required field.

[7.4] [LISTING]
A Comparison between Human and NLP-based Annotation of Clinical Trial
Eligibility Criteria Text Using The OMOP Common Data Model
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8378608/

  WHY IT MATTERS HERE
  Reports that nearly half of the discrepancies between the system and the
  gold standard were errors in the GOLD STANDARD itself. That is the strongest
  single reason the disagreement taxonomy carries an OLD_READER_ERROR class
  and does not assume the existing reader is right.

[7.5] [LISTING]
Text Annotation Handbook: A Practical Guide for Machine Learning Projects
arXiv:2310.11780
https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.11780

  WHY IT MATTERS HERE
  Practical rather than theoretical. Useful as a checklist against
  docs/annotation-spec.md before clinicians are asked to use it.

8. FABRICATION AND HALLUCINATION IN CLINICAL MODELS
   Not needed yet. Needed before the LLM phase, and it is the evidence base
   for the standing no-fabrication rule.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

[8.1] [VERIFIED]
MedHallu: A Comprehensive Benchmark for Detecting Medical Hallucinations in
Large Language Models
Pandit S, Xu J, Hong J, Wang Z, Chen T, Xu K, Ding Y
arXiv:2502.14302, 20 February 2025
https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.14302

  WHY IT MATTERS HERE
  10,000 question-answer pairs from PubMedQA with systematically generated
  hallucinated responses. The finding to carry forward: adding a "not sure"
  response category substantially improved detection. An abstention option
  improves a model's honesty about medical content - the same result the
  classifier's abstention policy is relying on.

[8.2] [LISTING]
Large Language Models Are Highly Vulnerable to Adversarial Hallucination
Attacks in Clinical Decision Support: A Multi-Model Assurance Analysis
Communications Medicine, 2025 (also medRxiv 2025.03.18.25324184)
https://www.nature.com/articles/s43856-025-01021-3
https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.03.18.25324184.full.pdf

  WHY IT MATTERS HERE
  The most alarming result for this project's use case. Six leading models,
  300 physician-designed clinical vignettes, each containing ONE fabricated
  element - a fictitious laboratory test, a fabricated physical or
  radiological sign, or an invented disease. The models repeated or elaborated
  on the planted error in up to 83 percent of cases. A mitigation prompt
  halved the rate but did not remove it.

  Read this as the justification for the architecture already chosen: a model
  that is handed a document and asked what is in it will confirm a value that
  is not there. Deterministic extraction with a character span is not
  conservatism for its own sake.

[8.3] [LISTING]
MedHallBench: A New Benchmark for Assessing Hallucination in Medical Large
Language Models
arXiv:2412.18947
https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.18947

  WHY IT MATTERS HERE
  A second benchmark with clinician validation. Useful as a comparison point
  to [8.1] on how medical hallucination is defined and scored, which is a
  precondition for defining it consistently in this project's own spec.

[8.4] [LISTING]
MHB: Medical Hallucination Benchmark for Large Language Models in Complex
Clinical Tasks
Proceedings of the AAAI Conference on Artificial Intelligence
https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/41243

  WHY IT MATTERS HERE
  4,695 samples and 20,288 evaluation rubrics validated by 60 licensed
  physicians, built by injecting hallucination traps into realistic medical
  data. The rubric-per-sample design is worth studying if the clinical
  reasoning benchmark is ever expanded.

9. BENCHMARK DESIGN AND REPORTING STANDARDS
   Behind the two-benchmark split and what a written-up pilot should contain.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

[9.1] [LISTING]
Reporting guideline for the early-stage clinical evaluation of decision
support systems driven by artificial intelligence: DECIDE-AI
Vasey B, Nagendran M, Denniston A, McCulloch P, and the DECIDE-AI expert group
Nature Medicine, 2022; 28:924-933 (published simultaneously in BMJ 2022;
377:e070904)
https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-022-01772-9

  WHY IT MATTERS HERE
  The 27-item checklist for exactly the stage between "the algorithm works in
  a notebook" and "there is a trial". If the shadow pilot is ever written up,
  this is the structure to write it against. Worth reading NOW rather than
  later, because several of its items concern data that has to be captured
  during the pilot and cannot be reconstructed afterwards.

[9.2] [VERIFIED]
TRIPOD+AI statement: updated guidance for reporting clinical prediction models
that use regression or machine learning methods
Collins GS, Moons KGM, Dhiman P, et al
BMJ, 2024; 385:e078378
DOI 10.1136/bmj-2023-078378
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11019967/

  WHY IT MATTERS HERE
  The prediction-model counterpart to [9.1], and the relevant one for the CVD
  engine rather than for the document parser. Supersedes TRIPOD 2015.

[9.3] [LISTING]
The TRIPOD-LLM reporting guideline for studies using large language models
Nature Medicine, 2024
https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-024-03425-5

  WHY IT MATTERS HERE
  The LLM-specific guideline. File it for the LLM phase.

[9.4] [VERIFIED]
Beyond the Leaderboard: Rethinking Medical Benchmarks for Large Language
Models
Chen W, Yu G, Cheung Y-F, Ding M, Liu J, Ma Z, Wang W, Shen L
arXiv:2508.04325, 6 August 2025 (revised 29 April 2026)
https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.04325

  WHY IT MATTERS HERE
  Argues that medical benchmarks are disconnected from clinical practice and
  that safety-critical evaluation - robustness and uncertainty awareness - is
  widely neglected. Proposes MedCheck, a 46-criterion framework across five
  stages of benchmark development.

  This is external support for the two-benchmark split. A single combined
  score is precisely the leaderboard artefact the paper is attacking, and
  "uncertainty awareness" is the property the abstention reason codes make
  measurable.

[9.5] [LISTING]
MEDIC: Comprehensive Evaluation of Leading Indicators for LLM Safety and
Utility in Clinical Applications
arXiv:2409.07314
https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.07314

  WHY IT MATTERS HERE
  Evaluates safety and utility as separate axes rather than collapsing them.
  The same separation this project is enforcing between "how often is the
  classifier wrong" and "how much does abstaining forgo".

10. AUTOMATION BIAS AND FAILURE TAXONOMIES
    Why a second opinion can make the record LESS safe, which is the whole
    premise of the unsafe-disagreement metric.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

[10.1] [LISTING]
Automation bias - a hidden issue for clinical decision support system use
Studies in Health Technology and Informatics, 2011
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21335682/

  WHY IT MATTERS HERE
  The foundational statement: users over-accept computer output as a heuristic
  replacement for vigilant information seeking and processing. If the second
  reader is ever shown to a clinician, this is the mechanism by which a
  machine that is right 95 percent of the time can still degrade decisions.

[10.2] [LISTING]
Automation bias in electronic prescribing
BMC Medical Informatics and Decision Making, 2017
https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5356416/

  WHY IT MATTERS HERE
  Measures automation bias directly, by counting clinicians switching from a
  correct prescription to an incorrect one after seeing incorrect system
  advice. That switch - correct to incorrect, caused by the system - is the
  human-facing version of what is being counted here as an unsafe
  disagreement.

  A caution on numbers. Reviews in this area report single-digit percentage
  switch rates, and one ECG study reports a fall in resident diagnostic
  accuracy when the system was inaccurate. Those figures come from several
  different studies; read the primary sources before quoting any of them.

[10.3] [LISTING]
Automation Bias in AI-Assisted Medical Decision-Making under Time Pressure in
Computational Pathology
arXiv:2411.00998
https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.00998

  WHY IT MATTERS HERE
  Adds the variable that matters in a real clinic and is usually missing from
  the studies: time pressure. Also reports that training and experience reduce
  false agreement with incorrect recommendations without eliminating it.

GAPS - WHAT I SEARCHED FOR AND DID NOT FIND
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  - Any published work on lakh-per-microlitre platelet reporting, or on
    automated handling of Indian-convention laboratory units generally. The
    unit-harmonisation literature is UCUM and SI centred. If this is solved
    here it appears to be new ground, and the conservative behaviour currently
    in place is the defensible default until someone measures it.

  - A published metric equivalent to "unsafe disagreements per 100 reports".
    The mammography literature counts arbitration RATE and change in
    sensitivity, not safety-directed disagreements. The closest formal framing
    is the risk-coverage curve in [3.1].

  - Any standard for what a shadow-mode disagreement taxonomy should contain.
    [1.2] states directly that no formal guidelines for silent evaluation of
    medical AI exist.
